Nathsagar — the Jayakwadi story

One of Asia's longest earthen dams holds back the Godavari at Paithan, watering the drought-prone Marathwada plateau, feeding a city, and — in its shallow backwaters — hosting one of India's great gathering places of migratory birds. This is its profile, its water record, and its trade-offs, with every figure sourced.

01The river and the reservoir

The Godavari leaves the Western Ghats and crosses the Deccan plateau. At Jayakwadi village near Paithan, an earthen barrier 10 kilometres long quietens it into Nathsagar — a backwater that stretches tens of kilometres upstream and shelters the Jayakwadi Bird Sanctuary on its shallow northern arms.

Schematic map. The dam's two canals carry water away from the Godavari into the interior of the drought-prone Marathwada region: the 208 km Left Bank Canal toward Jalna and Parbhani, and the 132 km Right Bank Canal toward Ahmednagar and Beed. [3][7][12]

03The water record, 2010–2024

Four views of the reservoir's pulse, drawn from a cleaned daily series (inflow and storage, 2010–2024) compiled from the CWC Dhalegaon gauge for this project — the same dataset the forecasting model trains on.

Days with zero recorded inflow, per year

The red bars are the honest caveat of this dataset: the upstream Dhalegaon CWC gauge reads zero on most days — partly the real dry-season silence of a river tapped upstream, partly gauge limitation. 2019 stands out as the year the gauge (and the monsoon) never slept.

Lowest storage reached each year (TMC)

In six of fifteen years the reservoir fell to 0.35 TMC — dead storage (2010, 2012, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2024). 2020 was the kindest year on record; 2024 ended at its floor again.

Highest daily inflow each year (m³/s)

2019's monsoon delivered the largest recorded pulse (2,151 m³/s). The zero bars of 2012, 2013 and 2015 mark the years the gauge recorded nothing at all.

The year's rhythm: storage by month (2010–2024)

The blue line is the average storage by month; the shaded band spans the driest to the wettest year for each month. The dashed red line marks the highest storage ever recorded in this window (76.6 TMC). The rhythm is flatter than most big dams — Jayakwadi's monsoon gift is spent within the same season.

04The sanctuary in the backwaters

Where the reservoir shallows into islands and mudflats, the Nathsagar Jayakwadi Bird Sanctuary was notified in 1986 — about 341 km² across Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar and Ahmednagar districts.

Who gathers here

  • 234–300+ bird species recorded across surveys (counts differ by method and year) [1][5][10]
  • Flamingos, painted storks, spoonbills, white storks, night herons, wagtails, pin-tailed snipe [1]
  • 67 fish species, 8 amphibians, 11 mammals documented in the 2023 conservation assessment [10]
  • ~30 roosting islands in the shallow waters [13]

When to watch

  • Migratory season: November–February, peaking December–January [14]
  • Water level matters: in nearly-full years the shallow mudflats submerge and counts drop; in drawdown years the flats expand and birds crowd in [15]
  • The sanctuary is still awaiting formal upgrade of its protected-area tag [16]

05Benefits and trade-offs

What it gives

  • Irrigation for ~1.83 lakh ha of the drought-prone Marathwada plateau via the 208 km LBC and 132 km RBC [1][2][7]
  • Drinking-water lifeline for Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad)
  • 12 MW of hydropower (commissioned 1976; offline since Dec 2023 for maintenance) [8]
  • Fishing economy and a noted birdwatching destination on the sanctuary's islands

What it costs

  • Siltation: the 2003–2012 survey found ~30–31% of capacity lost; a 14% (10.73 TMC) live-storage loss is cited [1][17]
  • Displacement: the 1965–1976 submergence moved villages along the Godavari; consistent public figures are scarce — flagged as a gap in this package
  • Drought exposure: in 2016 the reservoir touched 0.5% of live storage; May 2024 fell to 5.19%; Jul 2024 5.21% despite normal rain [18][19][20]
  • Canal ageing: the LBC's carrying capacity has roughly halved, with ₹3,000 crore repairs cited [21]
Flagged gaps: original displacement and village-submergence figures are not consistently published online; the WRD live-report text layer lists two candidate storage values (738.11 / 1,977.57 Mcum) whose column mapping should be confirmed on the portal; species counts vary 234–300+ by survey year and method.
